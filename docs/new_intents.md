# New intents: does the router notice, and how many labels does one need?

_Generated by `scripts/new_intents.py`. 3 random draws of 10 Banking77 intents held out of the published student (frozen MiniLM + LR on 3,000 teacher labels); mean ± sd over draws._

[Out-of-scope detection](oos.md) covered *far* out-of-scope traffic. Here the unknown messages are banking messages: the student is retrained without some intents, then shown their test queries, as if a new product had launched.

## Does it notice?

Four ways to score how unfamiliar a query is. The first two read the head's probabilities; the last two measure where the query's embedding lies relative to the training queries. Near = the held-out banking intents; far = CLINC150's 1,200 out-of-scope queries against the same student trained on all 77 intents.

| score | near: AUROC | near: caught at 5 % | near: caught at 10 % | near: caught at 20 % | far: AUROC | far: caught at 10 % |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| max probability | 0.828 ± 0.022 | 34% ± 6% | 52% ± 6% | 71% ± 4% | 0.967 | 91% |
| entropy | 0.825 ± 0.025 | 31% ± 5% | 53% ± 5% | 72% ± 6% | 0.980 | 95% |
| Mahalanobis distance | 0.719 ± 0.011 | 12% ± 2% | 27% ± 4% | 47% ± 5% | 0.997 | 100% |
| relative Mahalanobis | 0.847 ± 0.033 | 30% ± 9% | 54% ± 10% | 75% ± 9% | 0.964 | 92% |
| k-NN distance (k = 10) | 0.716 ± 0.021 | 8% ± 3% | 17% ± 2% | 39% ± 3% | 0.997 | 99% |

**Budget** = the share of in-scope (kept-intent) test queries escalated; the cell is the share of new-intent queries that threshold catches.

Where the new intents' queries end up (draw 0) — the kept intent that absorbs most of each:

| held-out intent | filed under | share |
|---|---|---:|
| `Refund_not_showing_up` | `request_refund` | 95% |
| `activate_my_card` | `card_linking` | 70% |
| `automatic_top_up` | `topping_up_by_card` | 42% |
| `card_about_to_expire` | `order_physical_card` | 48% |
| `card_payment_fee_charged` | `extra_charge_on_statement` | 40% |
| `card_payment_wrong_exchange_rate` | `wrong_exchange_rate_for_cash_withdrawal` | 48% |
| `direct_debit_payment_not_recognised` | `card_payment_not_recognised` | 72% |
| `failed_transfer` | `declined_transfer` | 88% |
| `pending_transfer` | `transfer_timing` | 42% |
| `top_up_reverted` | `top_up_failed` | 80% |

**Much harder than far out-of-scope.** With entropy, AUROC drops from 0.980 (far) to 0.825 (near), and a 10 % budget catches 53% of the new-intent traffic instead of 95%. The distance scores, the usual remedy, split the two cases the other way: best on far traffic (Mahalanobis distance 0.997, k-NN distance (k = 10) 0.997) and worst on near (0.719, 0.716). A new banking intent lies among the known ones in a general-purpose embedding space, so being far from the training queries is exactly what it is not; and the head splits its vote on it the way it does on a hard in-scope query, so probabilities cannot tell either. The variant built for this case, relative Mahalanobis, moves near AUROC by +2.2 pt over entropy (within the spread between draws) and far by -1.6 pt: no per-message score fixes this. What does give it away: the neighbours absorb it — on average 53% of a new intent's queries land in a single kept intent.

## The fix: watch the mix of intents, not each message

`distilroute/monitor.py`, served at `GET /monitor`: the first 5,000 routed messages set each intent's expected share; after that the latest window is compared with it intent by intent, and an intent whose share jumps (z > 3.65, the one-sided Bonferroni threshold for 1 % over 77 intents; the measured false-alarm rate is the last row) is flagged by name. The service's default window is 2,000 messages. Simulated here with every held-out intent in turn taking a share of the traffic (500 streams per cell, all draws): the share of streams flagged, and in brackets the share where the top flag is the intent the newcomer lands in.

| new intent's share of traffic | window 250 | window 500 | window 1,000 | window 2,000 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2% | **7%** (3%) | **10%** (7%) | **17%** (15%) | **30%** (27%) |
| 5% | **36%** (30%) | **59%** (52%) | **81%** (73%) | **94%** (86%) |
| 10% | **83%** (72%) | **97%** (86%) | **100%** (92%) | **100%** (95%) |
| 0 % (false alarms) | 3.9% | 3.5% | 2.8% | 1.6% |

A new intent is flagged in 90 % of streams at 5% of traffic within a 2,000-message window; at 10% of traffic within a 500-message window. Per message the router cannot tell (above); in aggregate it can, and it points at where the new queries are going. **Assumes** otherwise stable traffic: real traffic drifts by weekday and season, so in production the reference should be refreshed and the false-alarm rate checked on the service's own history.

## How many labels does a new intent need?

Each held-out intent added back with k examples (gold train rows, standing in for a person labelling k messages of the new intent); the rest of the pool is unchanged.

| k per new intent | new intents' accuracy | kept intents' accuracy | overall accuracy |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0.000 ± 0.000 | 0.862 ± 0.002 | 0.750 ± 0.002 |
| 5 | 0.394 ± 0.016 | 0.862 ± 0.003 | 0.802 ± 0.001 |
| 10 | 0.617 ± 0.017 | 0.863 ± 0.003 | 0.831 ± 0.001 |
| 25 | 0.791 ± 0.022 | 0.861 ± 0.003 | 0.852 ± 0.005 |
| 50 | 0.861 ± 0.014 | 0.854 ± 0.001 | 0.855 ± 0.003 |
| 100 | 0.912 ± 0.004 | 0.848 ± 0.001 | 0.856 ± 0.000 |
| all 77 from the start (~39 teacher labels each) | 0.818 ± 0.022 | — | 0.846 ± 0.000 |

**The all-77 student's 0.818 on these intents is matched at k = 50 labelled examples.** Five already route 39% of the new traffic correctly. The price is paid by the neighbours: kept-intent accuracy moves by -1.5 pt from k = 0 to k = 100, where each new intent has more rows than a typical kept one.

The all-77 row is the published student's configuration fitted on the whole pool (the published run holds 10 % back for calibration, hence 0.846 here vs 0.848 there); every row in this table is fitted the same way.

**Caveats.** Rows are held out by the *teacher's* label, so the few held-out-intent messages the teacher had filed elsewhere stay in the pool (as they would: a teacher without the intent in its list files them somewhere). The added examples are human labels, cleaner than the teacher's ~85 %, which flatters k slightly against the all-77 row. Intents with fewer than 120 gold train rows are never held out.
