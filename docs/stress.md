## The fix: route sentence by sentence

Split the message on sentence ends, route each sentence alone, answer with the most confident one (escalate if even that is below the threshold). Every sentence fits the 64-token window, and a sentence whose answer reaches 0.5 calibrated confidence counts as a detected intent — a cheap multi-intent signal. **naive** routes every sentence; **+ off-topic filter** first drops sentences whose entropy is above the level only 10 % of clean queries reach (the out-of-scope test of [6.2](oos.md), per sentence), and routes the whole message if nothing is left. Same models, no retraining.

**What it shows.** The served model falls from 0.867 to 0.013 when the question ends a 120-word ticket — it never reads past token 64 — though the cascade escalates 90% of those. The frozen MiniLM, which reads 256 tokens, keeps 0.763 on the same tickets, and routed sentence by sentence with the off-topic filter it keeps 0.837 (clean 0.847). Sentence routing helps most where the filter recognises chit-chat (TF-IDF is weak on short sentences either way) — whole message → sentence routing with filter, question at the end of 120 words: tfidf_lr_teacher 0.313 → 0.430 (1 of 18 filler sentences pass); minilm_frozen_teacher 0.763 → 0.837 (0 of 18 filler sentences pass); minilm_ft_teacher 0.013 → 0.550 (4 of 18 filler sentences pass); tinybert_ft_teacher 0.027 → 0.447 (6 of 18 filler sentences pass); distilbert_ft_teacher 0.023 → 0.770 (1 of 18 filler sentences pass). A student that is confidently wrong on small talk lets a filler sentence outscore the real question. It does work as multi-intent detection: see the last column against the whole-message top-2 above.

Pairs columns use the "Also," join, where the two questions are separate sentences (the plain join often has no sentence break to split on).

**Caveats.** The filler is written to be intent-neutral and is the same 18 sentences throughout; real tickets carry context that points *toward* an intent (and sometimes away from it). Joined pairs are two independent test queries, not how one person writes two questions. Both sets are rule-built, like the noise of [robustness](robustness.md).
