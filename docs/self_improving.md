# A router that learns from its escalations

_Generated by `scripts/self_improving.py` (banking77). The training split replayed as live traffic: a frozen-MiniLM student starts from 500 LLM-labelled messages, then 9 weeks of 1,000 messages arrive. Below 0.8 calibrated confidence a message goes to the LLM, and its answer is kept. Every arm is retrained at the end of each week. Accuracy against human labels on the 3,080-message test split; mean of 3 seeds (different traffic order and cold start). LLM teacher alone: 0.867._

## What it shows

Learning from its own escalations, the student goes from 0.704 to **0.852**, and the share of live traffic it sends to the LLM falls from 48% to 23%. Every label it learned from was an LLM call already made to answer a customer.

- **Against labelling everything** (the ceiling, 0.859): 0.7 pt short, with **31% of the LLM calls** (2,901 vs 9,500); the cascades score 0.873 and 0.873.
- **Against never retraining:** +14.8 pt, and *fewer* LLM calls (2,901 vs 4,888), because a static student keeps escalating 49% of traffic. Retraining pays for itself.
- **Against the same number of random labels:** +1.3 pt, and random labels cost 5,076 calls, since each one is bought on top of the escalations.

**Caveats.** Simulated traffic: the training split in random order, so no drift and no new intents. The frozen MiniLM stands in for the served fine-tuned model because it retrains in seconds on a CPU. The LLM's answers carry its own errors (13 % on the test split), and the escalated messages are where it errs most (finding 6), so the student learns some of those too.
